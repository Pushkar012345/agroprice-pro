# Storage and selling-timing guidance

## Hold or sell
Holding produce only makes sense if the expected price gain is larger than the cost of holding it. Count storage rent or your own storage cost, interest on money tied up, weight loss and spoilage, and the risk that the price does not rise. For perishable produce the risk is high, so selling soon is usually safer.

## Storage basics for onion
Onion keeps best when it is well cured and dry before storage, kept in a ventilated structure, and checked regularly so that rotting or sprouting bulbs are removed before they spread. Moisture and poor airflow are the main causes of loss.

## Storage basics for grains and pulses
Dry the crop to a safe moisture level before storing, keep it off the bare floor, protect it from pests and rain, and inspect it periodically. Damp grain spoils quickly and fetches a lower rate.

## Use price history carefully
A past rise in price does not guarantee a future rise. Compare the current price with the recent average, look at how volatile the price has been, and decide how much risk you can accept. Selling in parts, instead of all at once, reduces the risk of picking the wrong day.
