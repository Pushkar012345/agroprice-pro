# Minimum Support Price (MSP) basics
Source: https://agriwelfare.gov.in

## What MSP is
The Minimum Support Price is a rate announced by the Government of India at which it is prepared to buy certain crops from farmers. It is based on recommendations of the Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices (CACP). It acts as a safety net: if open-market prices fall below MSP, government agencies can procure the crop at MSP, subject to the procurement rules for that crop and state.

## Which crops are covered
MSP covers mainly cereals, pulses, oilseeds and some commercial crops such as cotton. Common examples are wheat, paddy, tur, soybean and cotton. Perishable vegetables and fruits such as onion, tomato and potato do not have an MSP, so their prices are set by the market.

## How it relates to mandi prices
MSP is not the same as the daily mandi price. For covered crops, MSP is a floor that the government offers through procurement. Mandi prices can be above or below it. A mandi modal price below MSP is a signal to check whether government procurement is open for that crop in your area.

## Where to find the current rates
MSP values change every season. Do not rely on remembered numbers. Check the latest announcement on the Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare website or with your local APMC or procurement agency.
