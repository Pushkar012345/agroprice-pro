# APMC mandis and e-NAM
Source: https://enam.gov.in

## APMC mandis
An Agricultural Produce Market Committee (APMC) runs a regulated wholesale market, called a mandi, where farmers sell to licensed traders and commission agents. The APMC records arrivals and prices, and charges a market fee. Mandi prices reported by the government come from these markets.

## Rules differ by produce and by state
Which produce must be sold through an APMC has changed over time and differs by state. In Maharashtra, fruits and vegetables were taken out of mandatory APMC regulation in 2016, but many farmers still sell them at APMC markets. Check the current rules with your local APMC office before assuming what applies.

## e-NAM
The National Agriculture Market (e-NAM) is an online trading platform that links APMC mandis, so a produce lot can be bid on by buyers from other markets. A farmer typically registers with identity and bank account details, brings the produce to a participating mandi, and the lot is graded and listed for online bidding. Payment is meant to go directly to the farmer's bank account. Only mandis that have joined e-NAM offer this.

## Practical tip
Ask your mandi whether it is on e-NAM and what the fees and payment timelines are, because these affect how much you actually receive compared with the quoted modal price.
